Caterpillar demand quality
Was Caterpillar's 2026 Construction Industries growth driven by real end-user
demand or dealer restocking?
Sam Felix · 5 October 2026 · Eight quarters, Q3 2024 – Q2 2026 · Sourced to Caterpillar and SEC filings,
with earnings-call commentary identified as such
Caterpillar's Construction Industries growth was still heavily supported by dealer restocking in Q1 2026, but by Q2
the mix had shifted materially toward end-user demand. The disclosed dealer-inventory build fell from $1.5B to
$400M, management changed its own explanation from inventory-first to end-user-first, and retail growth increased
from 7% to 22%.

The band is the signal. Where price-adjusted reported growth runs above retail growth, the
pattern is consistent with dealer inventory building; where it runs below, it is consistent
with slower accumulation or destocking. The signal is widest in Q1 2026 and narrows
sharply in Q2.
Q1 2025 is the control case
The same pattern runs the other way a year earlier, which is what keeps it from being a
story fitted to one quarter. Retail sales to end users rose 3% while price-adjusted CI
shipments fell 13.8%. End demand was growing while reported shipments collapsed, as
dealer inventory accumulation slowed sharply.
Retail corroborates; it does not carry the finding
Q2 2026 retail growth of 22% is one strong quarterly observation, not an established trend.
The finding rests on the filed dealer-inventory dollars and management's own wording,
with retail agreeing rather than leading.

FORWARD JUDGMENT
What this implies, and what would break it
If Caterpillar follows through on the expected dealer-inventory drawdown while end-user demand remains healthy,
dealer stocking should contribute less to reported CI growth in H2 2026. Reported growth should therefore move
closer to retail growth, or temporarily trail it during destocking.
Stated as inference, not finding. All three describe H2 2026, which had not been reported when this was written.
Strengthens the thesis
Retail growth remains elevated while dealer inventory growth continues to slow or turns negative.
Weakens the thesis
Retail growth falls materially in H2 while dealer inventories draw down, suggesting Q2's 22% was a
temporary spike rather than evidence of durable end demand.
Alternative: demand pull-forward
Positive tariff-related price realization may have encouraged some customers to purchase earlier than
planned, inflating Q2 retail at the expense of H2. A hypothesis, not a proven fact. H2 retail behaviour
should distinguish durable demand from pull-forward.
What I expected to corroborate — and didn't
I expected inventory-heavy Q1 2026 growth to show up as unusually weak cash
conversion. It didn't — Q1 is seasonally weak and Q1 2026 operating cash flow was
stronger than Q1 2025, so cash flow does not independently confirm the stocking thesis. It
is reported here because leaving it out would be selective.

MARGIN, LIMITATIONS, AUDITABILITY
Margin context, and what the analysis cannot claim
The Q4 2025 margin trough was a tariff cost shock rather than operating deterioration, and the 2026 recovery is
partly that shock receding.

Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 drags are dollar figures disclosed in the earnings releases. Q2 2026 is sourced as 340 bps
from the Q2 2026 earnings call and converted to dollars here. Ex-tariff margin is an upper bound on underlying
performance: the price realization that lifted it was taken partly to recover the same tariff costs. Separately, Q2
2026 consolidated operating profit included $392m of one-time IEEPA tariff recoveries, which the CFO stated sat
mostly in corporate items — CI segment margin is unaffected by them.
Caterpillar 4Q 2025, 1Q 2026 and 2Q 2026 earnings releases and Q2 2026 earnings call
Tariffs are not a one-time item — they recur while in force, guided to roughly $2.2bn for
full-year 2026, with management indicating roughly half of the Q3 burden falls in
Construction Industries. This is a cost shock receding and price recovering it, not an
efficiency or cost-control finding.
Limitations
The reported-vs-retail gap is directional and relative, not a precise dealer-inventory
dollar measure.
Q2 2026 retail strength is a single observation and needs H2 confirmation.
Segment-level dealer-inventory dollars are not disclosed continuously, so no unbroken
eight-quarter series exists at either scope.
Methodology and sources
Financial figures are sourced to Caterpillar and SEC filings wherever available: quarterly
earnings releases Q3 2024 – Q2 2026, Ex 99.2 Rolling 3-Month Retail Sales Statistics, and
the Q1 and Q2 2026 Forms 10-Q (accessions 0000018230-26-000021 and -000046).
Management commentary not reproduced in a filing is identified as earnings-call
commentary and supported by transcript sources. No third-party estimates or analyst
summaries are used as financial inputs. Where the 10-Q documents would not serve Item 2
on retrieval, figures were confirmed by exact-phrase match against the EDGAR full-text
index — present in the filing, though the surrounding sentence was not read.
Underlying workbook available for download — the eight-quarter dataset, 32
reconciled bridges, a source and classification for every figure, and the comparability log.
