Was 2026 Construction Industries growth driven by real end-user demand, or by dealer restocking?

Sam Felix · Eight quarters, Q3 2024 – Q2 2026 · Caterpillar earnings releases and SEC filings · Revised 14 October 2026

The question

Reported sales measure shipments to dealers, not demand from customers

Construction Industries reported sales grew 38.1% in Q1 2026 and 34.8% in Q2. Both figures count machines shipped to dealers.

Growth that stops at dealers reverses when they stop restocking; growth that reaches end users does not. In principle the gap between the two arrows tracks dealer stocking, but the two disclosures differ in price basis, scope, parts and services, and possibly currency, and dealer-inventory dollars are disclosed only in some quarters. So the comparison is a directional signal, not an accounting identity.

Demand quality

The shipment-to-retail gap narrowed sharply, from ~24 pts to ~8 pts

Price-adjusted CI shipment growth less retail growth to end users. A wide gap is consistent with dealers stocking; a narrow one with machines reaching customers. Retail growth is disclosed in whole percentages, so the gap is shown to the nearest point.

Q1 2025 is the control case

A year earlier the pattern ran the other way: retail sales to end users rose 3% while price-adjusted CI shipments fell 13.8%, as company-wide dealer stocking slowed sharply (+$0.1B against +$1.4B a year earlier; no CI figure was disclosed that quarter).

Operating drivers

Volume, not price, produced the 2026 growth

Price realization was negative in all six quarters from Q3 2024 through Q4 2025. Because volume carries the growth, where that volume went — to end users or into dealer inventory — decides whether it lasts.

Reconciliation caveat

In the workbook, volume, price, currency and inter-segment components reconcile to the reported CI sales change in seven of eight quarters. Q2 2025 closes by construction: its prior-year sales figure was back-solved from the bridge, so it is not an independent check.

Forward test and margin context

What would confirm or break the thesis, and what margin can and cannot show

H2 2026 tests (inference, not finding)

If dealer inventory stops building or draws down in H2 2026 while end-user demand holds, reported growth should move toward retail growth, or trail it during destocking.

StrengthensRetail growth stays elevated while dealer inventory growth keeps slowing or turns negative.
WeakensRetail growth falls materially in H2 while inventories draw down, suggesting Q2's 22% was a spike.
Alternative (hypothesis)Tariff-related price increases may have pulled purchases forward, inflating Q2 retail at the expense of H2.

What the analysis cannot claim

  • The gap is a directional signal, not an accounting identity or a dollar measure; the pressure test supports its sign, not its size.
  • Q2 2026 retail strength is a single observation and needs H2 confirmation. Cash flow did not independently confirm the Q1 stocking thesis.
  • Dealer-inventory dollars are not disclosed continuously at either scope, and Q1 is seasonally a build quarter.
  • Q2 2026 call guidance, not an observed outcome: Caterpillar expects “a more typical reduction in Construction Industries dealer inventory of over $1 billion in the fourth quarter” and full-year 2026 tariff costs of around $2.2 billion, excluding approximately $400 million of expected IEEPA tariff recoveries (CFO Kyle Epley).
