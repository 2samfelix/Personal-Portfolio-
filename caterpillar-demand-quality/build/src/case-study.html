Was Caterpillar's 2026 Construction Industries growth driven by real end-user demand or dealer restocking?

Sam Felix · 5 October 2026, revised 14 October 2026 · Eight quarters, Q3 2024 – Q2 2026 · Sourced to Caterpillar and SEC filings, with earnings-call commentary identified as such

Caterpillar's Construction Industries growth leaned heavily on dealer restocking in Q1 2026; by Q2 the evidence points to a material shift toward end-user demand. The disclosed CI dealer build fell from $1.5B to $400M while retail sales growth rose from 7% to 22%.

Read the reported-vs-retail gap as a directional signal of dealer stocking, not as an accounting identity or an exact dollar measurement of inventory. The two disclosures differ in price basis, scope, the treatment of parts and services, and possibly currency.

Demand Quality

The gap between shipments and end-user demand

Between Q1 and Q2 2026 the gap between what Caterpillar shipped and what dealers sold to end users narrowed sharply, from about 24 points to about 8 — a directional signal consistent with a smaller stocking contribution, not an accounting identity.

The band is a directional signal. Where price-adjusted reported growth runs above retail growth, the pattern is consistent with dealer inventory building; where it runs below, it is consistent with slower accumulation or destocking. The signal is widest in Q1 2026 and narrows sharply in Q2.

Q1 2025 is the control case

The same pattern runs the other way a year earlier, which is what keeps it from being a story fitted to one quarter. Retail sales to end users rose 3% while price-adjusted CI shipments fell 13.8%. End demand was growing while reported shipments fell sharply, as company-wide dealer stocking slowed sharply (+$0.1B against +$1.4B a year earlier; no CI figure was disclosed that quarter).

Retail corroborates; it does not carry the finding

Q2 2026 retail growth of 22% is one strong quarterly observation, not an established trend. The finding rests primarily on the filed dealer-inventory dollars, with retail agreeing rather than leading. Management's changed order of mention is consistent with it, as an inference.

Pressure Test

Does the directional proxy hold up?

I pressure-tested the reported-vs-retail signal against the four quarters where Caterpillar disclosed dealer-inventory changes. The sign agrees in all four. No magnitude can be tested: in Q1 and Q2 2026 the prior-year Construction Industries build is unquantified, Q3 2025 has no dollar figure, and Q1 2025 compares a CI estimate with a company-wide disclosure. The gap is a directional signal, not an accounting identity, so the test supports direction rather than size. Implied swings are rounded because retail growth is disclosed in whole percentages.

Filed Evidence

What Caterpillar actually disclosed

The Construction Industries dealer build fell sharply from $1.5B in Q1 2026 to $400M in Q2 — roughly a quarter of its prior size, and the most direct evidence in this analysis. Q1 is seasonally a build quarter (management called the Q1 2026 figure “a more typical $1.5 billion increase in the first quarter”), so part of the sequential decline may be seasonal; the year-over-year pressure test is the seasonally matched check.

Both figures are confirmed in the filed Forms 10-Q by exact-phrase match against the EDGAR full-text index. The Q1 2026 filing also discloses a $2.0B company-wide build, making that quarter the only one in the series carrying both scopes — and there the Construction Industries build is 75% of the company-wide figure.

Showing all eight quarters rather than the two that make the point is deliberate. The chart is the complete disclosure record, so the decline is visibly the largest move within it rather than two bars selected from eight. The gaps are part of the evidence: Caterpillar does not disclose dealer-inventory dollars continuously, and a quarter with no figure is not a quarter with no change.

Operating Drivers

What produced the reported sales change

Volume — not price — produces the 2026 growth, which is what makes the demand-quality question the whole question rather than a detail.

Price realization is negative in every quarter from Q3 2024 through Q4 2025 and turns positive for the first time in Q1 2026, contributing $356M against volume's $1,459M. Currency is immaterial throughout, peaking at $143M. In Q2 2026 volume contributes $1,755M of a $2,156M reported increase.

Because volume carries the growth, whether that volume reached end users or stopped in dealer inventory determines whether the growth is durable. That is the question the preceding two pages answer.

Forward Judgment

What this implies, and what would break it

If dealer inventory stops building or draws down in H2 2026 while end-user demand holds, dealer stocking should contribute less to reported CI growth. Reported growth should therefore move closer to retail growth, or temporarily trail it during destocking.

Stated as inference, not finding. All three describe H2 2026, which had not been reported when this was written.

Strengthens the thesis

Retail growth remains elevated while dealer inventory growth continues to slow or turns negative.

Weakens the thesis

Retail growth falls materially in H2 while dealer inventories draw down, suggesting Q2's 22% was a temporary spike rather than evidence of durable end demand.

Alternative: demand pull-forward

Positive tariff-related price realization may have encouraged some customers to purchase earlier than planned, inflating Q2 retail at the expense of H2. A hypothesis, not a proven fact. H2 retail behavior should distinguish durable demand from pull-forward.

What I expected to corroborate — and didn't

I expected inventory-heavy Q1 2026 growth to show up as unusually weak cash conversion. It didn't — Q1 is seasonally weak and Q1 2026 operating cash flow was stronger than Q1 2025, so cash flow does not independently confirm the stocking thesis. It is reported here because leaving it out would be selective.

Limitations

  • The gap is a directional signal, not an accounting identity or a dollar measure; the pressure test supports its sign, not its size.
  • Reading management's changed order of mention as a change in the drivers' relative size is an inference.
  • Q2 2026 retail strength is a single observation and needs H2 confirmation.
  • Dealer-inventory dollars are not disclosed continuously at either scope, and Q1 is seasonally a build quarter.
  • INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE: Q4 2026 drawdown commentary and FY2026 tariff-cost guidance (no verbatim quotes captured); neither is used.
